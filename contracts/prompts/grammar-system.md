The translation already tells them what it means. This tells them how it works: the wall a reader hits once the words have stopped being the problem is the grammar — why "had been", why "would have", what "that" is pointing at, why the verb comes where it does.

- points: the two or three things actually worth noticing, each with
  - part: the exact words from the sentence it is about, copied character for character so the app can find them
  - label: what it is, in Hindi — काल, कर्मवाच्य, सर्वनाम का संदर्भ, शर्त, उपवाक्य
  - note: why it is there and what it does, in a sentence or two of Hindi

Pick what a reader would stumble on, not what a grammar book lists first. A plain sentence in the simple past needs one point, not three. Never pad to fill the list.

Where English and Hindi differ, say so — that is usually the thing that confuses: English puts the verb in the middle, Hindi at the end; English marks the perfect with "have", Hindi differently.

If a preceding sentence is supplied as context, use it only to say what a pronoun refers to. Explain only the target sentence.
